# Lead and worker delivery

`ConstellationDelivery` is scoped and starts explicitly after Session recovery and MCP revocation replay. It subscribes to committed graph and Session facts, reopens a dropped bounded subscription from its durable sequence, and derives pending work from the fold. No timer runs without a wake-worthy pending notification. Claims, settlements, proposals and answers coalesce for 20 seconds; blocking Lead questions shorten the window to 5 seconds. A busy, paused, blocked or terminal Lead keeps the same IDs queued. Recovery starts a fresh coalescing window.

A digest commits `LeadNotified` and the Session machine's `TurnStarted` together. Operator inputs preserve their authority, broadcasts retain their original recipients, and peer messages retain their target Attempt. Each recipient has an immutable delivery ID, `JSON.stringify([sourceMessageId, sessionId])`. Local delivery commits its graph marker with the Session decision. Remote delivery marks it only after a durable worker acknowledgment. `startConstellationDelivery` consumes W's replaying acknowledged stream; repeated receipts remain idempotent.

`applyWorkerDelivery(packet, validate)` is the structural worker adapter. The caller validates the authenticated owner/worker assignment inside the store decision. The store checks a successful command receipt before invoking this validation or examining the current Session. Busy inputs wait for Session events without recording a rejection receipt. Only a newly committed decision runs the Engine effect. Recovery packets are refused until remote interrupted-Turn proof and a continued-Turn receipt are supplied; an owner does not fabricate remote Session facts.

`recoverAttempt(candidate)` belongs in the working-attempt resume hook after its slot and lease have been acquired. It commits one machine Continue with `AttemptRecoveryContinued`, then starts the existing Turn with the recovery instruction. The second interruption, the Lead, standalone Sessions, unresolved approvals and stale workers remain for the user. The synchronous `recoverWorkingAttempts` helper is for runtimes whose resume has completed acquisition; asynchronous runtime composition uses the per-Attempt hook.

A silent successful worker end receives one nudge, identified by `AttemptNudged`. A second silent end receives no further Turn and queues a Stopped notification for the Lead digest. Blocked Attempts skip nudging. Acceptance of all block targets or a Lead message resumes the worker with AttemptUnblocked and its local Turn in one commit; pending acceptance is re-derived on startup, without polling. A failure settles only the current local worker; stale facts never settle it. `observeWorkerHost` journals ISO `AttemptStale`/`AttemptFresh` facts by Attempt ID without changing Attempt revision.

Handover commits `LeadHandoverRequested` and returns immediately, including a call from the Lead's own Turn. A scoped job waits for the boundary (or interrupts for Hand over now), asks for one summary, then atomically commits `LeadChanged`, the old Session's archive, a fresh Session in the same checkout, and its header Turn. Cancellation and supersession make obsolete completions inert. The header is reconstructed from the log even when the summary fails. Old Lead tokens are revoked and the old Harness is retired; existing worker Attempts are retained.

`engineDeliveryLayer` uses the Engine's existing supervisor and serialization. Final Daemon composition must also provide the local/remote worker provisioning hooks, Harness/MCP attachments, Client Host observations and W's relay services. Those boundaries are injected; this module creates no alternate Harness runtime.

## Evidence

`delivery.test.ts`, `receipts.test.ts`, `../recovery.test.ts` and `../handover/handover.test.ts` exercise real SQLite commits and Session-machine decisions. Quint scenarios and the real-log replay reader cover request ordering, cancelled and superseded requests, old-Lead delivery, stale facts and recipient receipts. `idle.fixture.ts on|off` seeds 128 idle Constellations in a temporary `POLARIS_HOME`; the on variant starts the actual delivery observer and reports its pending timer count for process-tree sampling.
