# Design System: Polaris

Scope: the Desktop App (macOS), and the Mobile App in its own section at the end. Product truth, users, and voice live in `PRODUCT.md`; domain vocabulary lives in `CONTEXT.md`. This file never redefines either.

## Overview

**North star: "Night watch."** Polaris is the window a developer keeps open for hours while several agents work. The chrome is quiet, nearly colourless, and flat, so the code and the agents' work stay in front. Two things break the quiet, and only when they mean something:

1. **Signals.** Colour appears only for Session State, Severity, diffs, and Harness identity. Motion appears only while an Agent Session is Working.
2. **The pixel world.** Pixel icons on watercolour-washed tiles, dither that shimmers in a Harness's hue while it works, and full pixel scenes (a night sky with Polaris in dark mode, a meadow at dawn in light mode) in onboarding and empty states.

Dark is the default theme; light is equally supported. Every token exists in both, and neither is an afterthought.

The Desktop App is Electron (Chromium 152, React). CSS effects are available: shadows, rounded clipping, per-element `backdrop-filter`, springs, custom fonts, and window-level vibrancy. The budgets still rule: display-rate frames (180 Hz measured) and under 1 GB for the whole app. See **Performance** under Motion.

**Assets and source files.** Generated textures live in `design/assets/` (dither frames, state icons, scenes, watercolour washes, halos) and are reproducible from `design/scripts/gen_dither.py` and `design/scripts/gen_textures.py`. Mockups: the Paper file "Polaris — Orchestrator" (https://app.paper.design/file/01M3JV1J857QNW61TGHZYR6GMZ); artboard 5, "Orchestrate · Elevated", is the chosen Orchestrator direction, artboard 7 shows a constellation with a subagent in focus, the Review page holds R1 (pull request, dark) and R2 (agent session turns, light), with M2's proposed states in R3 (pull requests, dark), R4 (review checkout menu, dark), R5 (review checkout states, light), R6 (comment composer, dark), R7 (submit review, dark) and R8 (agent session feedback, light), and the Editor page holds E1 (editor, dark), E2a (selection, dark), E2 (inline chat, dark) and E3 (an agent editing your file, light). The Updates page holds U1–U6, proposed: Settings → About with an update ready (dark) and with automatic checks off (light), the update row's states, the K menu and the app menu with an update ready, a host's "Upgraded to 0.5.0" in the machine bar (light), and Settings → Hosts after Daemon Upgrades. The file's Paper tokens mirror this frontmatter as `-dark`/`-light` pairs.

**Mode:** Operate. Scanability, consistency, and native macOS expectations outrank expression. Brand lives in precise details and the pixel world.

### Brand

- **Wordmark:** "Polaris" in Geist Pixel, style Square (from the `geist` package, SIL OFL), title case. It appears only where Polaris names itself: the title bar (13px, after the traffic lights, beside the 16px star), About, onboarding, and app icon lockups. Never in working UI copy.
- **Mark:** a symmetric 16×16 pixel star: a 2px spine, rays that step out evenly (2, 4, 6, 8, 16 pixels wide), and short diagonal rays, in the spirit of Minecraft's Nether star. At 24px and up it is shaded in three tones (outline, body, white core): `design/assets/brand/polaris-logo-*.svg`. Below 24px it is flat: `design/assets/icons/px-polaris-*.svg`. - **Idea and tagline:** Polaris is the fixed point your agents steer by. Tagline: "The north star for your agents." Principles: calm, precise, alive.
- **Lockups:** horizontal (default), stacked, and mark only. Clear space on every side equals a quarter of the mark. The star always leads the wordmark.

### Installer

The DMG window: the "Installer" page of the Paper file. **I3 is the design** (the dawn scene); I4 checks it under Finder's dark appearance. I1 (night) and I2 (its light-appearance check) are history, marked superseded. Drag the app to Applications under the dawn sky; no wordmark and no copy.

- **Window:** 660×400 points of content (the background size; Finder's title bar sits above it), toolbar, sidebar, path bar and status bar hidden, icon view, icons 100pt, labels 12pt at the bottom, no item info. The volume is named "Polaris" and its volume icon is `Polaris.icns`.
- **Icon centres** (points from the content's top-left, as `.DS_Store` stores them): Polaris.app at **(165, 190)**, the Applications alias at **(495, 190)**. Both labels sit on sky: the far ridge rises just right of the Applications label's last letter (from 254pt at x 526) but never under the text.
- **Finder labels are black on a custom background (the finding):** a real mounted DMG shows Finder draws icon labels black over a background picture in both light and dark appearance; only the window chrome follows the appearance. So the night art (I1) left the labels unreadable for everyone, not just in light mode. Decision (owner, 2026-10-02): the dawn scene for everyone, since black text reads on it in both appearances.
- **Background (`gen_dmg.py`):** the dawn scene drawn natively at 330×200: `scene()` in `gen_textures.py` with the dawn sky and hills, star `#FFFFFF`, `night=False` and seed 12; then the meadow flowers (`random.Random(4)`, `round(260 × 330×200 / (360×225))` = 212 of `#F4D35E`, `#FFFFFF`, `#F2B5C4` in the bottom 12% of rows), then the cabin with `CABIN_DAWN` seated by `place_cabin` in the 0.14–0.34 band. Scaled by nearest neighbour ×2 for 1× (660×400) and ×4 for 2× (1320×800), so one scene pixel is 2pt. Polaris lands at (474, 72), a faint white star in the sky above Applications; the cabin sits under the app. There are no stars to clear at dawn.
- **Arrow:** centred between the icons at scene cell (165, 95), 48 cells long, in neutral ink `#4A4C52` (6.9:1 on the sky around it; never Starlight, rule/starlight-is-rare): a one-cell dashed shaft (2 on, 1 off, from cell 141) into an open chevron head at cell 188 whose arms step five cells back in a two-cell stair. Drawn into the background on whole scene pixels; never anti-aliased.

## Colors

### Primary

**Starlight** (`starlight-dark` #BCD3FF, `starlight-light` #4F82E8) is the brand: a cool white-blue. It appears in exactly four places: keyboard focus rings, the activity indicator and dither for Polaris's own work (such as the Risk Summary reviewer), the logo mark, and the star in the night-sky scene. Where Starlight is set as words (the Starlight kicker over a scene), it uses `starlight-text`: the same #BCD3FF in dark, and #2F5FC4 in light, because #4F82E8 is 3.5:1 on the dawn clearing (rule/scene-text-contrast).

### Neutral

Faintly cool greys (a trace of blue, never readable as blue). Dark background is a soft charcoal (#1A1B1D), never pure black; light background is off-white (#FBFBFC).

- Hierarchy comes from **text colour**, not weight: `text-strong` for titles and selected items, `text-default` for content, `text-subtle` for secondary labels, section headers, metadata, ages, second lines and key hints, `text-faint` for placeholders and disabled text only.
- **rule/faint-is-not-content:** `text-faint` is for placeholders, disabled controls, ignored files and ghost text, never for text the user must read. It is below 4.5:1 in both themes by design; anything informative (ages, counts, empty-state lines, line numbers, key hints) is at least `text-subtle`, which clears 4.5:1 on every surface in both themes.
- Layers: panels on a ground (Chrome redesign): `ground` under the title bar, bars and sidebar, `panel` for the conversation, Output, Review's centre, the editor and Settings' page, `well` inset into a surface, `surface-raised` for popovers, instrument cards and toasts. `surface-sunken` and `bg` remain only as aliases older surfaces still read.
- Selection is a soft grey fill (`fill-selected`), never an accent colour.
- Borders are 1px `hairline` (white or black at ~6%).

### Named Rules

- **rule/colour-means-something:** a colour on screen must be Starlight, a Session State signal, a Constellation state (rule/constellation-colour), a Severity, a diff, a git status, a syntax token, or a Harness hue. Anything else is a neutral.
- **rule/syntax-is-moonlit:** syntax colours stay low-chroma and never reuse a signal hue, so a Severity, diff or Needs You mark is always the loudest thing over code.
- **rule/git-is-a-letter:** a git status is always its letter (M, A, U, D, R, C, !) in a fixed slot, with the file name tinted to match. It never uses a dot or icon alone, so modified tan never reads as Needs You yellow (which is always a pixel icon).
- **rule/constellation-colour:** inside a Constellation (the tab, its sidebar group, Claims and its Needs you cards) each Task state reads at a glance: working keeps the Harness hue on its dither (and in the progress strip), done with evidence is `accepted` on glyph, id and state word, needs you is `needs-you`, a failed check is `failed-text`; in review, waiting and Futures stay neutral. `accepted` appears nowhere else, and Starlight stays out of it.
- **rule/starlight-is-rare:** Starlight never appears on buttons, links, selection, or hover, with two exceptions: it fills New session and Send (Chrome redesign, Controls), and the Editor's INSERT badge. The focus ring stays Starlight.
- **rule/no-colour-alone:** every colour signal also has an icon, shape, or label.
- **rule/signal-text-variants:** a signal hue set as words (a label, a second line, badge text, a count, a git letter, the danger button) uses its `-text` variant, which clears 4.5:1 on `bg`, `surface-sunken`, `surface-raised` and its own badge fill in that theme. Glyphs, washes, dither, rules and line fills keep the base hue. Where a pair has no `-text` variant (needs-you, severity-medium and failed in dark; diff and git-modified in dark), the base value already clears 4.5:1 and is the text colour.
- **rule/severity-is-a-badge:** a Severity is always a filled badge with its shape (◆ ▲ ● ○), its label, and its colour. Never colour alone, never a bare dot.
- **rule/severity-vs-state:** Severity badges and Session State indicators never share a form. State uses 16px pixel icons and dots with no label; Severity uses labelled badges. This keeps Medium yellow apart from Needs You yellow and Critical red apart from Failed red.
- **rule/low-confidence-dims:** non-Critical Findings under 50% confidence render at reduced emphasis (text-subtle, badge at 60%). Critical is never dimmed.

## Elevation & Depth

Flat. Depth comes from panels on a ground (`ground` < `panel` < `surface-raised`, wells inset one step off their surface) plus 1px hairlines; on light, panels add `shadow-panel`. Only floating things cast a float shadow.

### Blur and vibrancy

- **Window vibrancy** (native, behind content) is allowed for the title bar and sidebars only, and must look right with vibrancy off.
- **`backdrop-filter` blur** is allowed on floating layers: menus, popovers, the jump menu and its scrim, toasts. Keep the radius at or under 20px, never animate the blur radius, and never put a blurred layer over content that is scrolling or animating underneath.
- Working surfaces (lists, conversation, diff, Editor) are opaque.

### Shadow Vocabulary

- **float:** menus, popovers, command palette, toasts. A soft, low-contrast shadow (dark: y 8, blur 24, black 40%; light: y 8, blur 24, black 10%) plus a hairline.

### Copy button

Every copy in the app is one component, `CopyButton` in `@polaris/ui`, writing through the Desktop App's main process (the renderer has no clipboard permission). Three variants: **inline**, a small fill beside the text it copies (an install command: `fill-selected`, `caption`, 22px, 4px radius); **icon**, a 16px Nucleo copy icon in a ghost square at the top right of a code block's header (a table, which has no header, gets a 28px strip above it), its name in a tooltip; and **button**, a Button keeping its variant ("Copy code", "Copy command"). After a click it says "Copied" in `text-strong` (the icon becomes a check), or "Couldn't copy" in `failed-text` (the icon a cross) when the write fails, for 1500ms, then its label again; another click restarts the 1500ms. A polite live region says the same. No Starlight: copying is not a signal (rule/starlight-is-rare). Motion: a press dips the button to 97% over 120ms; the label and its answer share one grid cell, so the width never changes, and cross over 160ms (opacity, and a scale from 90%), all ease-out, as a transition that a new click retargets mid-way. Reduce Motion switches the text instantly with no dip. A copy from a menu item ("Copy path") answers in a 1.5s toast instead: "Copied path", or "Couldn't copy".

The main unit of the app. A row is an optional 16px icon or tile, a `label` title, and optional `caption` metadata on the right. Hover gives `fill-hover`, selection gives `fill-selected`, both at the `row` radius, inset from the panel edge by the panel padding. Sidebar section headers are `caption` in `text-subtle`, with no dividers.

- **Shortcuts.** Right-aligned, `caption` (12px) in `text-subtle`, always formatted by the app's chord formatting (`formatChord`) from KEYMAP, `EDIT_CHORDS` (⌘X/C/V/A in the composer and terminal) or a feature's own key table (the explorer's F2 and ⌘⌫, the rail's A, S and ↵); never typed by hand. A chord shows only where it acts on the menu's target: ⇧⌘A on the selected session's card, ⌘N and ⌘3 on the current Workspace's chip.
- **Hidden, not dimmed.** An action that can't apply to this target is left out. A row is dimmed, its reason in the shortcut lane ("Host offline"), only where its absence would confuse.
- **Destructive.** Only what Polaris can't undo reads in `failed-text`: Delete and Discard changes…. Archive and Dismiss stay neutral.
- **Keyboard.** ↑↓ move, ↵ or Space choose, esc closes, typing jumps to a row; ⇧F10 or the menu key opens the menu on the focused row (sidebar, file tree, tabs). It fades in over 160ms and closes at once.
- **rule/ellipsis-means-input:** as on macOS, a menu item or command title ends in "…" exactly when it asks for more before it runs (a dialog, picker, inline field or confirmation): Rename…, New file…, Fork…, Discard changes…, Attach files…. One that acts at once never has one: Archive, Copy path, Open in terminal, and the explorer's Delete, which moves to the Trash at once (it asks only on a Host with no Trash). A submenu's chevron is not an ellipsis ("More models", not "More models…"). Items declare it (`needsInput`) and KEYMAP titles declare it (`needsInput`), and `shell/menu/ellipsis.test.ts` fails when a label and its declaration disagree, or a label uses three dots.

### Pixel tiles

### Working strip

While a Turn runs, the composer grows a 34px strip across its top: a dither glyph, a working verb ("Flat out…") in the Harness hue, elapsed time in `text-subtle`, then, while any run, the running command or how many ("· running bun test" for one, truncated to its first line and the strip's width; "· 2 commands running" for more; same style; the Turn's and its Subagents' commands in flight plus the session's background commands, such as Claude Code's background shells and Codex commands the model moved on from, each counted once), and a Stop control (esc) on the right. The strip has no band of its own (no sunken fill, no rule under it): it sits on the composer's surface, and its only ground is a fine dot field in the Harness hue: 1px dots on a 2px grid over a faint tint (dots ~29%, tint ~9% at rest), densest behind the glyph and label at the top-left and thinning out (dots drop by an ordered Bayer threshold) by ~150px right and the strip's bottom. Light theme raises the dots to ~1.25× and lowers the tint so they read at the same weight. The composer's border takes the Harness hue at ~28% with a 3px outer ring at ~6%. This strip is the loudest thing on a Working screen and replaces any spinner.

- **The field answers the pointer and the status.** While the pointer is over the Working composer, a small patch (~22px radius) of sparse `text-strong` dots on a staggered 8×4px lattice follows it (in over 90ms, out over 160ms), and the hue dots brighten in a trail behind it that fades over ~0.4s. When the strip appears or its status line changes, the corner blooms: it floods out to ~1.3× its reach over 400ms, then settles to rest over 900ms. While the Turn runs the field is live (`LiveDither`, ENG-514): its Bayer threshold walks one step and its edge breathes (±7% reach over ~2.5s) at 12 fps, on one timer that runs only while the field is active, in view and the window shown (rule/only-working-moves). It is drawn on a canvas at one pixel per CSS px, painted only on a live step or while the patch, trail or bloom moves: no frames when no Turn runs, and none under Reduce Motion, where the field is the still resting corner. The live Tool Call row carries the same field, without the bloom.

- **The working verb** rotates, as ChatDCA's does: a random first verb, then the next every 6s, faded in over 160ms (opacity only); Reduce Motion swaps it instantly and keeps rotating, since a text change isn't motion. It never re-blooms the field. Screen readers hear "Claude Code is working" instead of each verb. Claude Code sessions use `spinnerVerbs` from Claude Code's own settings on that Host, as Claude Code applies them (`~/.claude/settings.json` < the project's `.claude/settings.json` < `.claude/settings.local.json`; `replace` uses only those, `append` adds them after Polaris's). Otherwise, and for every other Harness, Polaris's built-in list, which the user edits in Settings → Sessions → Working verbs. A verb without an ending gets "…".
- **Keys while a Turn runs.** ↵ steers the Turn in flight; ⌘↵ queues the draft as a follow-up that is sent as the next Turn when this one ends. The placeholder says both: "Steer this turn · ⌘↵ to queue a follow-up". A Harness that can't be steered takes ↵ as queue, and the placeholder reads "Queue a follow-up for after this turn". Steers and queued follow-ups show at once at the end of the conversation as the user's own bubble at 60% opacity, with a caption: "Steering…" until the Harness takes the steer, then it sits in the Turn where it landed as a full bubble captioned "Steered this turn"; "Queued for after this turn" with Cancel (back into the draft) until the follow-up starts the next Turn, whose prompt it becomes. One that doesn't land (refused, or the Turn ended first) stays at full opacity with a pixel failed glyph, the reason in `caption`, Edit (back into the draft) and Retry ("Send as next turn" for a steer whose Turn is over). Nothing the user sends ever disappears.
- **Sent messages come back with ↑.** With the caret on the prompt's first line (no menu open, no modifier), ↑ replaces the draft with the last message the user sent in this session (Turn prompts and steers, newest first; a Turn the Harness or Polaris started, or Review feedback, is skipped); ↓ on the last line goes forward again, and past the newest the unsent draft comes back as it was. Typing in a recalled message makes it the draft. The caret lands at its end, so a multi-line message is walked line by line before the next one.
- **While a question is open,** the composer answers it in free text: "Or answer in your own words". While an approval is open it is locked with "Answer the request above to go on".

### Sources

The selected session's Sources show as chips, not rows: a brand mark tinted by its own brand (Linear indigo, GitHub neutral) plus the identifier, files in `code-inline`, and a dashed "+ Add" chip. Brand colour here is the brand's, not Polaris's, and stays at chip scale.

### Waiting on background tasks

A Harness can leave work running after its Turn ends (Claude Code's background agents, `Bash` with `run_in_background`): each is a background task, a subagent or a command. The Session State stays Idle, but an Idle session with live background tasks reads as waiting on them, never as idle, and it is not a needs-you (rule/needs-you-is-loudest). A command the user moved there with Run in background (Turns in the conversation) joins them like any other.

- **Words.** "Waiting on 2 subagents", "Waiting on a command", "Waiting on 1 subagent and 1 command": a lone task of one kind is "a", and numerals for both kinds once they mix. The sidebar row's second line, the session header's state ("… · turn 8") and the Output rail's state fact say it in place of "Idle". Compact drops the second line, so its row says "waiting" where the age was. Hovering any of them lists each task: its description (commands in `code-inline` mono), then its kind in `text-subtle`, wrapped to two lines at most.
- **Glyph.** The tile keeps the Idle dot: the glyph is the Session State (rule/severity-vs-state), and waiting is not one. The conversation closes with the same words in `caption` `text-subtle` with a dotted underline (the hover), after a 12px *still* Harness-hue dither in the avatar column: the Harness has work out, and nothing loops (rule/only-working-moves).
- **A Turn the Harness started itself (owner, 2026-10-19).** When background tasks report, the Harness may start a Turn without the user. In the Desktop App a Turn runs from one user message to the next, so it folds into the Turn before it: no Turn card, fold or Turn number of its own, and no rail tick. Inside that Turn a `caption` `text-subtle` line on the agent's side says what woke it and the Model ("2 subagents reported · Opus 5.5 · high", "A command finished · Default · 14:25", "A subagent reported and a command finished"; "Continued on its own" when the Harness names none), then its items under the Harness avatar. The Turn covers all of it: folding folds it all, the folded summary (last reply, files, +/−) and the diff's Turn menu (one entry per Turn, its diff from the first checkpoint to the last) span it, find opens it, "Copy turn output" copies every reply in it, and while the Harness runs it the Turn is the latest, so nothing in it folds by itself. Only a Harness-started Turn with no Turn before it (a session's first) stands as a Turn of its own, opened by that line with the fold control, and its summary falls back to the same words. The Daemon, its events and checkpoints keep each Turn as it is; older pages that begin inside a Turn load the page before at once, so a Turn is never split or miscounted. The compatibility prompt ("[Background task continuation]") is never shown.
- **Turn numbers (owner, 2026-10-19).** Turn numbers count these Turns, the user's, never the Daemon's: the conversation and its rail, the sidebar's "Idle · turn N" and "Working · turn N", Review's section dividers, turn picker and "turns N–M", "Accept turn N" and "Accept through turn N", review comments' "Sent with turn N" and "Goes with turn N", the Constellation digest card, the prompts in Review's Overview, and the editor's agent strip. A Daemon with `session.user-turns` numbers them itself, so a long session is numbered exactly before its older pages load; from an older Daemon the Desktop App counts what it has listed. "Accept through turn N" accepts through the last Turn the Harness ran in Turn N: a continuation belongs to the Turn the user asked for, and while it still runs Accept waits as for any running Turn.
- **Turn notifications.** "Finished a turn" waits for the session to settle with no background tasks left and fires once for the user's Turn; a Turn the Harness started continues it and never notifies on its own (owner, 2026-10-19; Settings → Sessions). A settle counts once it has held for 3 seconds, so a continuation that starts as the last task ends never gets an early banner; after a reconnect the Daemon's user Turn keeps a banner already shown from firing again, and the duration counts from the user's message (owner, 2026-10-19; ADR 0073).

### Dither halo

The only way Polaris adds glow or emphasis: an ordered-dither radial field in a single hue (Starlight or a Harness hue), drawn as pixels at ~35% alpha. Glow is always a dither halo, never a blur or a soft coloured shadow, because the pixel field is the brand. Used as the Working strip's field (see Working strip) and around Polaris in scenes. Never behind text that must be read.

### Dither

The signature component: a field of square cells lit on a strict pixel grid.

- **Geometry:** cells are 2pt squares on a fixed grid, lit by an ordered (Bayer 4x4) threshold pattern. No blur, no anti-aliasing, no gradients.
- **Timing:** a new frame every ~83ms (12 fps), so it reads as pixel animation, not video. It fades in over 200ms on entering Working and fades out over 200ms on leaving it.
- **Colour:** the Harness hue, or Starlight for Polaris's own work.
- **Implementation:** frames of lit cells baked into a horizontal sprite strip, stepped by translating the strip (`transform`) inside a clipped box, so the animation stays on the compositor. Canvas is acceptable for large fields such as scenes and for the Working strip's pointer field, which follows the pointer at display rate (on the cell grid) rather than at 12 fps.
- **Where:** the Working indicator, the composer while a Turn is being sent, mode or Harness activation moments, and 12px marks in the Editor's file tree and tabs on files a Working agent is changing. Never in the Editor's text area or behind diffs.
- **Reduce Motion:** replaced by a still pixel pattern.

### Pixel scenes

Full-bleed pixel illustrations: a night sky with Polaris for dark mode, a meadow at dawn for light mode. Only in onboarding, first run, and empty states (including the new-session page), never behind working surfaces (Orchestrator lists, Review, the Editor), except the horizon along the window's foot (Chrome redesign). Content over a scene sits on a `surface-raised` card. The current scenes are generated by `design/scripts/gen_textures.py`; do not copy the inspiration images. Both scenes carry the same cabin, seated on the ground line: moonlit with a lit window at night, warm timber with the lamp still on at dawn.

- **Ambient life.** Scenes are alive, quietly: a few stars twinkle at a time (at most three, each brightening one or two palette steps, settling, dimming toward the sky and settling again over 3–5s); the cabin's lit window and door gap breathe through four levels of their own lamplight, one level at a time (two slow waves of 3.7s and 6.1s with a rare brief dip, never a strobe); single smoke pixels rise a pixel a second from the chimney and drift with the wind as they fade. Now and then a visitor crosses: at night a meteor (a 40–70 pixel whole-pixel streak at one in two with a three-tone tail in the star palette, 25–70s apart), at dawn a V of three or five 3×2 birds flapping through three frames, a pixel every 110ms (40–90s apart). Visitors only take paths through clear sky, above the hills and clear of the headline, the setup card and any other content; with no clear path they skip their turn.
- **How it is drawn.** Everything animated comes from the scene's own map (`design/assets/scenes/scene-*.json`, written by `gen_textures.py` beside the PNG) in the scene's own colours, on one scene-sized canvas (one canvas pixel per scene pixel) scaled and placed exactly as the background is. It steps twice a second (a meteor or birds step faster while they cross), paints only when a pixel changes, and stops entirely when the window is hidden, the scene is off screen, Reduce Motion is on, or nobody has touched the window for 3 minutes (it wakes on the next input). Working surfaces never show a scene, so they never pay for it.

- **rule/scene-text-contrast:** any text set directly on a scene sits on a "clearing": a solid radial vignette of the scene's darkest colour (dark: `#070912` at ~90% in the centre, fading to 0) sized to the text block plus 80px, and secondary lines use `text-default`, not `text-subtle`. Every line must clear 4.5:1 against the worst pixel behind it, stars included.

### Updates and daemon upgrades

An Update is the Desktop App replacing itself with a newer Release; a Daemon Upgrade is a host's daemon replaced by the build the app carries (CONTEXT.md). Copy keeps the two words apart, lowercase: "update" for the app, "upgrade" for daemons. Mockups: Updates page, U1–U6 (proposed; the quiet upgrades and "Quit and update" approved by the owner, 2026-10-02).

- **Never interrupts.** An update downloads in the background and waits. There is no toast, badge, dot, dialog or Dock bounce, and nothing appears over a running Turn: the user finds it where they already look for the app's own state, and acts when they choose.
- **"Restart to update" in three places, all at once.** The app menu, under About Polaris: "Restart to update to 0.5.0" (the same slot reads "Check for updates…" otherwise), and Quit reads "Quit and update" (⌘Q installs on the way out; closing the window still only hides Polaris; kept by the owner, 2026-10-02). The K menu: an action "Restart to update" ("Polaris 0.5.0 · agent sessions keep working"), found by "update", "restart" or "upgrade", and listed last under Actions on an empty field; "Check for updates" and "Settings: About" sit beside it. The menu bar star's menu stays as it is.
- **A restart keeps sessions.** Agent sessions run in their daemons, so a restart never stops a Turn; the copy says "agent sessions keep working", never asks to confirm, and never counts down.
- **Quiet daemon upgrades (U5, U6).** After an automatic Daemon Upgrade the host says so where hosts already speak: the machine bar's caption under the host's name becomes "Upgraded to 0.5.0" (in place of its workspace count) for an hour or until the user opens that host, and its tooltip reads "Daemon upgraded to 0.5.0 · 3m ago" over "From 0.4.0, to match this Mac. Agent sessions kept working."; Settings → Hosts keeps "Upgraded to 0.5.0 · 3m ago" with the pixel check for a day. ### Shell commands in the composer (ENG-388)

Claude Code's bash mode, in the session composer. Built, not drawn in Paper.

- **Entering.** A `!` typed at the start of the draft turns the composer into shell mode where the Host has `session.shell-command` and the session's Harness runs shell commands (`HarnessModels.runsShellCommands`; Claude Code only, never by Harness name in the renderer). The text turns mono, and a Shell mode strip takes the Working strip's place (owner, 2026-10-20): the same 34px band and dither field in `shell` magenta (Claude Code's bash-mode pink; the composer's edge tints `shell` too), a still dither glyph (rule/only-working-moves), "Shell mode" in `shell-text`, then "· runs in `~/code/polaris`" in `text-subtle` with the directory in mono, and "esc to leave" at the right. Its tooltip reads "Runs in your login shell; Claude Code reads the output and replies". Queued behind a Working Turn it reads "· runs after this turn, in `~/code/polaris`" and keeps the Turn's Stop in place of "esc to leave". `shell` is used nowhere else. Elsewhere a `!` is just text.
- **Leaving.** esc drops the `!` and keeps the rest of the draft; deleting the `!` (Backspace on `!` alone) leaves it the same way. While a Turn works, esc stops the Turn as before.
- **Running.** ↵ runs the command in the session's directory, in the user's login shell (on a Windows Host, Git Bash as Claude Code's own bash mode finds it, else PowerShell), as a Turn of its own: its prompt is the command, and the agent replies once it has read the output. Staged attachments stay in the composer for the next prompt. `!` alone runs nothing. ↑ brings a sent command back in shell mode.
- **While a Turn works.** A shell command never steers: ↵ (and ⌘↵) queue it as a follow-up, and the cue reads "! Shell command · runs after this turn, in `~/code/polaris`". It waits, shows and can be cancelled like any queued follow-up, and runs when the Turn ends.
- **In the conversation.** The command sits in the user's lane where the prompt bubble would, as a command row on the prompt's fill (`fill-selected`, `card` radius) reading "! git status", with its output under it, and it folds like the agent's commands (open while running, open when failed). The agent's reply follows as usual. Never the raw `<bash-…>` tags.
- **When it doesn't run cleanly.** A non-zero exit shows its exit code with the output, and the agent still replies. One that can't start, or runs past 10 minutes, fails with the reason in its output. Stop kills the command and ends the Turn Interrupted; the agent doesn't reply. A steer sent while the command runs waits and reaches the agent after the output.

### Motion

Quiet. Hover is instant. Press is 120ms. Small reveals are 160ms. Panels and toasts are 200ms. All use ease-out. The tokens are `--duration-press`, `--duration-reveal`, `--duration-panel` and `--ease-out`, which is also every transition's default curve. A press dips a button to 97%. ### Performance

- Animate `transform` and `opacity` only. Anything that triggers layout or paint per frame (width, height, top, box-shadow, blur radius) does not animate.
- Dialogs and the jump menu are opaque `surface-raised` cards at every moment, their input row included: the card scales in from 98% over 200ms and closes at once, and only the scrim fades. Nothing behind ever shows through a dialog, even mid-animation.
- The Working dither steps a sprite strip by `transform`.
- Long lists and diffs are virtualised; nothing animates on scroll.

### Constellation (DAG)

A native take on herdr-dagr (ENG-169), specified by the Constellations v1 map (ENG-232; events ENG-236, tools ENG-237, a worker's life ENG-238). Terms are in `CONTEXT.md`: a Constellation belongs to one **Lead** Agent Session and holds Tasks; each try at a Task is an Attempt carried by a worker Agent Session; a worker's **Claim** puts its Attempt in review until the Lead or the user accepts it or sends it back; a Task may declare an **Area**; Gates are fan-in Tasks the Lead carries itself; Futures are intent for work not yet started (a worker can propose one); mapped unstarted Tasks are ready or waiting from their dependencies, with no separate Future state; Subagents nest under their session. Mockups: Paper page "C1 · Constellation per Lead", C1–C9 (artboard 7 on the Orchestrator page is the earlier per-Workspace baseline).

- **Where.** One Constellation tab per Lead, in that Lead session's output pane, next to its conversation. A Workspace can run several Constellations at once, each under its own Lead. There are no terminal panes for workers: peeking into one is the focus swap below. With a worker focused, the tab bar carries "Message lead (M)".
- **Header.** The Constellation mark, its name, "led by this session · running" (states: planning, running, paused, completed, archived), a **progress strip** of one 10×4px segment per Task in its state colour (pending proposals are outside the Task strip; past about 40 Tasks it becomes one proportional bar; hovering a segment shows a tooltip with the Task's id in mono, its title and its row's state word), and the next thing that needs you as a needs-you chip ("B5 stopped without reporting back", tab to jump).
- **Rows, not a canvas.** A rail list in the spirit of `git log --graph`: the trunk, collapsible groups with trailing counts ("1 needs you · 2 in review · 2 working", "3 done" in `accepted`), Tasks, and the Subagents under their Attempt. Fixed lanes: rail (60px), id in `code-inline`, title, actor (harness · model, "lead" for a Gate, "codex · on devbox" for a remote worker), state. The id lane is one width per view (`--id-lane`), fitted to its longest id in mono characters (at most 18 in the tab and digest); ids never wrap, a longer one truncates with the full id on hover, and second lines and indents start on the title lane. A group heading sits on the trunk with its chevron in the rail, its label on the id lane and its tally ending on the state lane, the same height as a Task's first line. A row is one line, plus at most one or two short `caption` lines when they carry state: never prose (rule/no-dashboard-clutter). A Claim's checks wrap onto another caption line rather than clip, each check whole (a long label truncates to the line). Below 26rem the state lane narrows to 5rem so the rail never scrolls sideways at the Output's 360px minimum.
- **Tree.** Groups are the outermost level; parent Tasks nest under them to any depth, a child taking its root's group. The rail draws it as `git log --graph` does: each depth is a column stepping right from the trunk, an elbow into each child's glyph, the parent's line running on past siblings still to come. The step is one per view, fitted to its deepest row (17px for one level, at least 7px), so the fold chevron keeps its place at the rail's right; rows deeper than the rail can draw (past four columns at 7px) stay on its last column and indent their content 0.75rem per extra level. Ids, titles and state words keep their lanes at every depth. A parent row is its id and title on their lanes, its leaves' tally ending on the state lane ("1 working · 2 blocked · 1 done"), the rollup's glyph (as a group's) and a chevron; it has no Attempt, actor or menu, and ↵, ←, → fold it like a group (← from a child selects its parent). Parents and groups count leaves only: the strip, the filters, Needs you and the tallies never count a parent itself. A parent opens by default; in a large Constellation it folds unless it holds something that needs you or is blocked, and the same rule opens a group. Jumping to a Task opens its group and every parent above it.
- **Blocked.** A worker that reports it can't go on reads "blocked" on the state lane in `text-default`, with the blocked glyph: a `text-subtle` ring crossed by a short bar (not the needs-you hand, not a diamond; rule/severity-vs-state). It is neutral: waiting on another Task's work, or on the Lead, is not waiting on the user. Its second line is "blocked by F1b" with each id in its state's tone, then the reason in `text-faint`; with no Tasks named it reads "waiting on the lead" and the reason. A blocked Task never reads "stopped without reporting back", and only a pending approval or a question to the user makes it needs you. Tallies say "2 blocked" after working (waiting is only counted when nothing is active); the header strip draws a blocked segment as `text-subtle` hatching, apart from waiting's flat faint fill. The digest's Blocked line uses the same glyph and words.

Words use the `-text` variants; every colour keeps its glyph (rule/no-colour-alone).

### Editor

The third mode (title-bar switch: Orchestrate, Review, Edit). Two zones: the explorer (264px, on the ground) and the editor pane (a panel; Chrome redesign, Editor). The code is the content; chrome stays neutral and quiet.

- **Explorer.** Workspace header (name, Host and path) and a Files / Changes segmented control, then the file tree in tree rows. Each row has two fixed trailing slots: an agent slot (a 12px dither in the Harness hue while a Working agent is changing that file, or the pixel needs-you hand on a folder where an agent is blocked) and a git slot (the status letter, or a 5px `git-modified` dot on a folder that contains changes). Below the tree, "Agents in {workspace}" lists that Workspace's sessions as session rows; the footer keeps the jump hint. An opened chain of lone folders reads as one row ("src/orchestrator"), as VS Code's compact folders. Deleted files stay in their folder, struck through, until committed. A collapsed folder takes the hand for anything blocked inside it, and a top-level file takes it on its own row. Changes is one flat list: the name (tinted), its folder in `text-subtle`, then the same two slots. "Agents in {workspace}" shows three sessions, needs-you first, then "N more". Right-click or the header's + offers New file and New folder; rows add Rename (F2), Delete (⌘⌫) and Copy path. Delete moves the file to the Host's trash without asking. Only on a Host with no trash does a dialog ask "Delete {name} for good?", with Cancel focused. A Worktree or Review Checkout can take the explorer's root ("Open in the editor" from Review); the header then shows its path and "‹ Back to {workspace}". Ignored files and folders (`git.status` with `ignored`) keep their place in the tree with the name in `text-faint`, no letter, and never count as changes.
- **Changes tab with `git.write` (Paper 15, 15b).** A commit box (the message, then one primary "Commit N files ⌘↵" counting staged files, off with no message or nothing staged; there is no stage-all-and-commit fallback), then "Staged N" and "Changes N" sections with Unstage all / Stage all; a file staged then edited shows in both. A row's hover actions are − (unstage) on Staged, and Discard and + (stage) on Changes. Discard always asks in a popover anchored to the row, Cancel focused: a tracked file goes back to its staged or committed version ("This can't be undone"), an untracked one moves to the Host's Trash (a Host without one asks "Delete for good?" as Delete does). When an open file under the root has unsaved edits, a caption under the button reads "{name} isn't saved · Save first"; it warns, it doesn't block. Committing runs the Host's own hooks: the box and rows lock, "Running hooks · Ns" and the hooks' output stream into a code well under the button. A refusal reads "Commit failed (exit N)" in `failed-text` with the output and Copy; the message and staging stay. A commit lands silently: the message clears and an empty tab says "Committed · {subject}". Daemons without `git.write` keep the read-only flat list. Pull and push sit above the commit box (Sync with the remote, below); there is still no branch switching here, and Accept pushes for sessions.
- **Write a message (Paper 15c, 15d).** A 22px ghost sparkle button in the Message field's bottom-right corner drafts a message from what Commit commits, the staged diff only, read on the Host; ⌥⌘G (Ctrl+Shift+G on Windows and Linux) or the palette's "Write commit message" does the same. Off with nothing staged ("Stage changes to write a message" on hover) and while committing. Writing streams into the field read-only, the footer reads "Writing · {model} · Ns" with Stop (esc), and Commit is off; Stop puts back the earlier text. A written message replaces the field with "Replaced · Undo" in its footer until the user types. A refusal shows under Commit in `failed-text`, naming the Harness and Host ("Claude Code isn't signed in on Linux VM."), with Sign in in terminal when that's the fix, Change model (Settings → Editor) and Dismiss; the message stays. The field owns the focus ring (on the wrapper, so it wraps the footer too), and the Changes tab's scroll area keeps 4px above it so the ring is never clipped. Settings → Editor's "Commit messages" row holds the Reviewer's Harness and Model chips without Default or Effort; Claude Code defaults to Haiku 5.5, Codex to GPT 6 Luna, and switching Harness picks its small Model.
- **Git status.** Modified `M` (`git-modified`), added `A` and untracked `U` (`diff-added`), deleted `D` (`diff-removed`, name struck through), renamed `R` (`git-modified`), conflicted `C` (`diff-removed`), ignored (`text-faint`, no letter). Letters and the tinted file name use the `-text` variants (`git-modified-text`, `diff-*-text`). The file name takes the same tint unless the row is selected. In the gutter, a 2px bar marks changed lines (`git-modified` or `diff-added`) and a small `diff-removed` wedge marks where lines were deleted.
- **Diff tabs (accepted, 2026-10-13).** Clicking a changed file in Changes opens its diff in a tab, VS Code's way: a preview tab (italic, replaced by the next one) until double-clicked, titled "main.ts (Working Tree)", or "(Index)" for a staged row. Staged compares HEAD with the index, Changes the index with the working tree, and the flat Changes list of a Host without `git.write` (and a conflicted file) HEAD with the working tree; added, deleted and renamed files diff against nothing or the old name. A 32px caption row: the path crumbs and "Index ↔ Working Tree" in `text-subtle`, then "N of M changes" with up/down chevrons (F7 / ⇧F7, the palette's Next change / Previous change), Unified / Split, the Syntax-aware switch, and a ghost Open file. The body is Pierre on the Review card, with unchanged context collapsed into "N unmodified lines" separators that expand. Syntax-aware (on by default; it and Unified/Split are a per-viewer setting) matches lines by token rather than by character: a line that only gained tokens stays one context row with a 2px `diff-added` bar, and only the changed tokens take the emphasis fill (`diff-*-emphasis`), drawn as CSS highlights so no DOM is added. ### Edit Predictions (accepted (owner, 2026-10-15))

Paper's Editor page holds EP1–EP8b next to TC1–TC5b, each in dark and light, drawn on the real `packages/client/src/prediction/score.ts` (lines 1–35, breadcrumb "client / src / prediction / score.ts › targets", status item "Tab completion: Zeta 2.1 on Mac Studio · 1.5 s"). **What an edit looks like (EP1, EP2, EP3, EP3b).** The current line is `text-default` on `fill-hover` with the 2×18px `text-strong` cursor, as v1. Removed characters within a line keep their syntax colour, struck through, on `diff-removed-emphasis` (dark `#F8514940`; light `#CF222E24`, twice `diff-removed-bg-light`, pending a real token); the new text follows straight after as ghost text in `diff-added-text` (dark `#57C46A`, light `#1A7F37`) with no fill. The `-cvd` diff tokens replace both colours when the colourblind palette is on.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:

- Use glossary terms exactly, in lowercase: "needs you", not "waiting"; "agent session", not "thread".
- Let neutral chrome carry the layout, and save colour for signals.
- Check every screen in both themes, at all three densities, and with the colourblind diff palette.
- Keep one primary button per view.
- Let a Harness's hue identify it everywhere it appears (dither, tile, toast, and the Working strip and Harness picker), and nowhere else.

### Don't:

- Use purple or neon gradients, gradient text, or glass effects (the "AI purple gradient" anti-reference).
- Put cards, charts, or panels on a view just to fill space.
- Animate anything other than the Working dither in a loop, outside a pixel scene's ambient life.
- Use Starlight on buttons, links, or selection.
- Put pixel scenes or dither behind code, diffs, or Orchestrator lists.
- Use blur as glow or emphasis; glow is a dither halo. Blur only separates floating layers (see Elevation).
- Use bold or semibold weights, all caps, emoji, or exclamation marks.
- Show a Severity as colour alone, or let anything hide or dim a Critical Finding.

### Codex fast mode

The Harness picker offers a neutral lightning-bolt checkbox, "Fast mode", for Codex on a Daemon with `session.service-tier`. Its hint says "Faster responses, higher usage cost". Picks commit with the Model and effort when the menu closes; Escape cancels. A lightning bolt in the closed chip identifies an enabled selection, also named in its accessible label. `/fast` toggles the same selection without sending a Turn. Changes apply from the next Turn and are unavailable during a Turn or when Model changes are blocked. The selection follows the Agent Session across resume and same-Harness Forks.
